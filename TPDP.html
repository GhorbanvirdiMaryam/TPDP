My experience

In June 2025 I travelled to Mountain View for TPDP 2025, the Theory and Practice of Differential Privacy workshop, hosted at Google. TPDP brings together people who prove results about differential privacy and people who deploy it in real systems, which made it the right place for my work: using differential privacy to protect the timing behavior of real-time systems.

The day before the workshop I picked up my printed poster in Mountain View. In the poster session I presented Schedule Indistinguishability: Applying Differential Privacy Models to Protect Runtime Timing Behaviors, joint work with my advisor Sibin Mohan. I explained to the DP community why a scheduler's timing can leak information, and why Gaussian noise under Rényi differential privacy held up better than Laplace noise in our experiments.

About the work

  • Real-time systems are predictable, so an attacker can learn private system state from scheduling patterns.
  • Earlier DP-based scheduling adds Laplace noise, which is not enough against long-term observation.
  • We add Gaussian noise to task inter-arrival times, guided by Rényi differential privacy.
  • Tested in a client-server setting where the attacker can watch timing or inject fake tasks.
  • Result: without noise, fake tasks help the attacker; with Gaussian noise under Rényi DP, that advantage drops sharply.

Authors: Maryam Ghorbanvirdi, Sibin Mohan · The George Washington University
